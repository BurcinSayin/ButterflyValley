# Inquisitor
Grim and determined, the inquisitor roots out enemies of the faith, using trickery and guile when righteousness and purity is not enough. Although inquisitors are dedicated to a deity, they are above many of the normal rules and conventions of the church. They answer to their deity and their own sense of justice alone, and are willing to take extreme measures to meet their goals.

## During combat encounters ... 

You balance between casting spells and attacking with weapons, typically the favored weapon of your deity. Because your spells per day are limited, you rely on specific focus spells given by your deity. When necessary, you know how to win a fight without magic.

## During social encounters ... 

You are a voice of your ideal, and the voice of your deity. Because you’re wise, you also pick up on falsehoods others tell. You don't shy away from deception or even intimidation if it helps you serve your deity, and come closer to your ideal.

You follow the tracks of those that are in opposition to your ideals, or are enemies of your deity. But you are also able detect nearby magic or interpret any religious writing you might come across.

# Class Features 

You gain these abilities as an inquisitor. Abilities gained at higher levels list the required levels next to their names. 

## Ancestry and Background 

In addition to the abilities provided by your class at 1st level, you have the benefits of your selected ancestry and background, as described in Chapter 2. 

## Initial proficiencies 

At 1st level you gain a number of proficiencies that represent your basic training. These proficiencies are noted in the start of this class.

## Deity

Your deity grants you the trained proficiency rank with the deity's favored weapon. If the favored weapon is uncommon, you also get access to that weapon. When selecting an alignment, you can pick one that is within one step of your deities allowed alignments. 

### Anathema

You're not as tied to the tenets of the deity as a cleric, but an inquisitor must still hold certain guidelines in high regard, despite that fact they can go against them if it serves the greater good of the faith. For these borderline cases, you and your GM determine which acts are anathema.

If you perform enough acts that are anathema to your deity, or if your alignment changes to be one that is more than a step away from one not allowed by your deity, you lose the magical abilities that come from your connection to your deity. The class features that you lose are determined by the GM, but they likely include your divine spellcasting. These abilities can be regained only if you demonstrate your repentance by conducting an atone ritual.

## Ideal

Inquisitors are privy to special lore not open to priests and other agents of their religion. These divine pursuits, called ideals, grant inquisitors the tools necessary for the fight against enemies of the faith.  

At 1st level, your ideal grants you a free trained skill, a skill feat to go with it, and an additional spell for your spell repertoire. You also gain the minor ideal benefits, which at first level includes a new focus spell for you to use against your enemies, or on your allies. 

Inquisitor ideals are described after the class features section. 

## Judgement Spells
Your deity's power grants you special divine spells called judgement spells, which are a type of focus spell. It costs 1 Focus Point to cast a focus spell, and you start with a focus pool of 1 Focus Points. You refill your focus pool during your daily preparations, and you regain 1 Focus Point by spending 10 minutes using the Refocus activity to pray to your deity or do service toward their causes.

Focus spells are automatically heightened to half your level rounded up. Certain feats can give you more focus spells and increase the size of your focus pool, though your focus pool can never hold more than 3 Focus Points. 

You gain the *baleful bane* judgement spell, a cantrip that makes the weapon you're holding more deadly and allows it to engulf your target in positive or negative energy upon inflicting a Critical Hit with a Strike. You are trained in divine spell attack rolls and spell DCs. Your spellcasting ability is Wisdom.

> # Judgement trait
>
> You can cast only one judgement spell each turn, and you can only sustain one of these at a time. Any ongoing effects from your previous judgement spell end immediately if you cast another one that requires sustaining.

## Divine Spellcasting

Your Deity and ideal provides you with divine magical power. You can cast spells using the Cast a Spell activity, and you can supply material, somatic, and verbal components when casting spells.  Because you're an inquisitor, you can draw replacement sigils with the tip of your weapon or your free hand for spells requiring material components, replacing them with somatic components instead of needing a material component pouch.

At 1st level, you can cast up to two 1st-level spells per day. You must know spells to cast them, and you learn them via the spell repertoire class feature. The number of spells you can cast each day is called your spell slots. As you increase in level as an inquisitor, your number of spells per day increases, as does the highest level of spells you can cast, as shown on Table 1–2: Inquisitor Spells per Day. Some of your spells require you to attempt a spell attack roll to see how effective they are, or have your enemies roll against your spell DC, wisdom is your modifier for this.

### Heightening Spells
When you gain spell slots of 2nd level and higher, you can fill those slots with stronger versions of lower-level spells. This increases the spell's level, heightening it to match the spell slot. Many spells have specific improvements when they are heightened to certain levels.

### Cantrips
A cantrip is a special type of spell that doesn't use spell slots. You can cast a cantrip at will, any number of times per day. A cantrip is always automatically heightened to half your level rounded up—this is usually equal to the highest level of spell you can cast as a inquisitor. For example, as a 1st-level inquisitor, your cantrips are 1st-level spells, and as a 5th-level inquisitor, your cantrips are 3rd-level spells.

## Spell Repertoire

The collection of spells you can cast is called your spell repertoire. At 1st level, you learn two 1st-level divine spells of your choice and five divine cantrips of your choice. You choose these from the common spells from the divine list or from other divine spells to which you have access. You can cast any spell in your spell repertoire by using a spell slot of an appropriate spell level. Your spell slots and the spells in your spell repertoire are separate. If a feat or other ability adds a spell to your spell repertoire, it doesn't give you another spell slot, and vice versa.

You add to this spell repertoire as you increase in level. Each time you get a spell slot (see Table 1–2: Inquisitor Spells per Day), you add a spell of the same level to your spell repertoire. At 2nd level, you select another 1st-level spell. At 3rd level, you add the first 2nd-level spell to your repertoire. At 4th level you gain your second and your spell repertoire reaches its maximum size of five spells.

At 5th level, in addition to adding two 3rd-level spells to your repertoire, you lose your lowest level of spell slots. Any time you lose a level of spell slots, you lose two spells in your repertoire as well. These can come from spells you already know or out of the number of new spells you're learning. On levels in which you don't change your spell slots, you can swap out multiple spells, as described below.

### Swapping Spells In Your Repertoire
As you gain new spells in your repertoire, you might want to replace some of the spells you previously learned. Each time you gain a level and learn new spells, you can swap out one of your old spells for a different spell of the same level. 

If it's a level at which you lose a set of lower-level slots, you can replace the two in either order. You can also instead swap a cantrip. You can also swap out spells by retraining during downtime.

At 6th level and every even level thereafter, you can swap out any number of your spells for different spells of a level you can cast. When you do, you must keep at least one spell you can cast with your lowest level of spell slots so you don't end up with slots you can't use. For instance, at 6th level you would need to keep at least one 2nd-level spell, but all your other spells could be 3rd level.

## Inquisitor Feats 2nd

At 2nd level and every even-numbered level thereafter, you gain a inquisitor class feat.

## Skill Feats 2nd

At 2nd level and every 2 levels thereafter, you gain a skill feat. You must be trained or better in the corresponding skill to select a skill feat.

As an inquisitor, you also gain additional skill feats at certain levels from your Inquisitions. 

## General Feats 3th

At 3rd level and every 4 levels thereafter, you gain a general feat.

## Skill Increases 3th

At 3rd level and every 2 levels thereafter, you gain a skill increase. You can use this increase to either become trained in one skill you're untrained in or to become an expert in one skill in which you're already trained.

At 7th level, you can use skill increases to become a master in a skill in which you're already an expert, and at 15th level, you can use them to become legendary in a skill in which you're already a master.

As an inquisitor, you also gain additional skill increases at certain levels from your Inquisitions. 

## Alertness 3th

You remain alert to threats around you. Your proficiency rank for Perception increases to expert.

Inquisitors are skilled at intimidating their foes. You gain the Stern Gaze reaction, which allows you to to take a stern look at a foed and fill them with fear.

All of your spells are signature spells. That means that if you know a spell, you can heighten it freely by casting it from a higher-level spell slot, up to the maximum level of spell you can cast. You can similarly cast any of its lower-level versions without learning them separately.

## Ability Boosts 5th

At 5th level and every 5 levels thereafter, you boost four different ability scores. You can use these ability boosts to increase your ability scores above 18. Boosting an ability score increases it by 1 if it's already 18 or above, or by 2 if it starts out below 18.

## Ancestry Feats 5th

In addition to the ancestry feat you started with, you gain an ancestry feat at 5th level and every 4 levels thereafter.

## Initial Inquisition 5th

You set out on an inquisition to come closer to realising your ideal. Choose an Initial Inquisition, you gain its associated focus spell. Inquisitions are described below the Ideals section.

Additionally, you gain an additional skill increase, which you can use to become trained in a skill, or become expert in a skill you are already trained in. You also add a skill feat that you meet the prerequisites for. 

## Weapon Expertise 5th

You've dedicated yourself to learning the intricacies of your weapons. Your proficiency ranks for simple weapons, martial weapons in the bow and firearm categories, unarmed attacks and your deity' favorite weapon increase to expert.

## Great Fortitude 7th

Your physique is incredibly hardy. Your proficiency rank for Fortitude saves increases to expert.

## Weapon Specialization 7th

You can inflict greater injuries with your favored weapons. You deal 2 additional damage with weapons and unarmed attacks in which you're an expert. This damage increases to 3 if you're a master, and 4 if you're legendary.

## Resolve 9th

You've steeled your mind with resolve. Your proficiency rank for Will saves increases to master. When you roll a success on a Will save, you get a critical success instead.

## Expert Spellcaster 9th

You've learned to better control the power your deity has granted you. Your proficiency ranks for divine spell attack rolls and spell DCs increase to expert.

## Advanced Inquisition 11th

You come closer to realizing your Ideal. Choose an advanced inquisition or initial inquisition, you gain its associated focus spell. 

Additionally, you gain an additional skill increase, which you can use to become trained in a skill, or become expert in a skill you are already trained, or become master in a skill you are already expert in. You also add a skill feat that you meet the prerequisites for. 

## Divine Smite 11th

As you grow closer to realizing you ideal, you become more refined in your use of divine energy and judgement spells. You no longer need to critically hit with a  Strike, or get a critical failure on save, to gain the additional effect on spells with the judgement trait (like the persistant damage on baleful bane). A regular success for Strikes, or a regular failure for saves, is enough to engulf your target in energy damage. 

Additionally, your eyes pierce right into your target's mind. The circumstance bonus from Stern Gaze increases to +2.

## Medium Armor Expertise 11th

You've learned to defend yourself better against incoming attacks. Your proficiency ranks for light armor, medium armor, and unarmored defense increase to expert.

## Weapon Mastery 13th

You fully understand your weapons. Your proficiency ranks for simple weapons, martial weapons in the bow and firearm categories, unarmed attacks and your deity' favorite weapon increase to master.

## Stalwart 15th 

If you picked Strength as your key ability score, your proficiency rank for Fortitude increases to master. If you picked Dexterity as your key ability score, your proficiency rank for Reflex increases to master. 

When you roll a success on the saving throw that you increased with this class feature, you get a critical success instead.

## Greater Weapon Specialization 15th

Your damage from weapon specialization increases to 4 with weapons and unarmed attacks in which you're an expert, 6 if you're a master, and 8 if you're legendary.

## True Inquisition 17th

Your Ideal is within reach. Choose a true inquisition, advanced inquisition, or initial inquisition, you gain its associated focus spell.

Additionally, you gain two additional skill increases, which you can use to become trained in a skill, become expert in a skill you are already trained in, or become master in a skill you are already expert in. You can not use both skill increases to increase the same skill. You also add a two skill feats that you meet the prerequisites for. 

## Vigilant Senses 17th

Through your adventures, you've developed keen awareness and attention to detail. Your proficiency rank for Perception increases to master.

## Master Spellcaster 17th

You fortify your spells with masterful prowess. Your proficiency ranks for divine spell attack rolls and spell DCs increase to master.

## Medium Armor Mastery 19th

Your skill with light and medium armor improves, increasing your ability to avoid blows. Your proficiency ranks for light and medium armor, as well as for unarmed defense, increase to master.

# Ideals

As an inquisitor, you select an ideal at 1st level. This ideal describes what you strive for how you view those in opposition of your deity and your values. An ideal grants you you a minor ideal benefit at level 1, a skill feat and makes you trained in a the mentioned skill. Your ideal also adds a spell to your spell repertoire that normally is not available to the divine tradition, but for you counts as a divine spell. 

If you are already trained in the skill given by your minor ideal benefit, you instead become trained in a skill of your choice. Likewise, if you already know the skill feat, you select another 1st level skill feat for which you meet the prerequisites. 

## Conversion

You are a powerful persuader. A honeyed tongue empowered by divine argumentation sways the indifferent and adversarial to your side. You're more inclined to turn others to your side, than you are to destroy them. But you don't shy away with causing confusion or infighting before you do so. 

**Skill Training** You become trained in Diplomacy.

**Skill Feat** You gain the *Group Impression* skill feat.

**Additional Spell** You add *befuddle* to your spell repertoire. 

### Minor Ideal Benefits: Conversion 

You confuse your enemies with words you have carefully chosen. You gain the *inspired rhetoric* focus spell. A spell that weakens the will saves of those who's mind are brought to doubt by your words.

## Damnation 

The prospect of an afterlife filled with torment can cause even the most stalwart to blanch, at least for a moment. You're extravagant, and a skilled performer, you're more inclined to sow chaos and leave your enemies in disarray, than you are to help or destroy them.

**Skill Training** You become trained in Performance.

**Skill Feat** You gain the *Impressive Performance* skill feat.

**Additional Spell** You add *lose the path* to your spell repertoire. 

### Minor Ideal Benefits: Damnation 

You frighten a creature you can see with an eternity of suffering. You gain the *fear the flames* focus spell. A spell that might even burn enemies as fear overtakes them. 

## Heresy 

Often it is hard to tell heretics from the faithful. You use duplicity, stealth, and the heretics' own arguments to root them out and bring them to justice.  You're more inclined to destroy those that oppose your deity, or your values, than you are to save or disrupt them. You prefer to use deception to bring down your enemies from within.

**Skill Training** You become trained in Deception.

**Skill Feat** You gain the *Lengthy Diversion* skill feat.

**Additional Spell** You add *phantom pain* to your spell repertoire. 

### Minor Ideal Benefits: Heresy 

You poison and sicken your enemies before picking them off. You gain the *poison the well* focus spell. A spel that deals bludgeoning damage in a small area, before unleashing toxins and acids in a larger range, potentionally sicking those caught in the blast. 

## Zeal 

An inquisitor needs unyielding zeal in order to make sure its deity's work is done. You're so occupied with your own ideals, so convinded of how much tey're worth, that you can't possibly fathom anyone who might oppose them. You're inclined to brag , and talk about them all the time, and care very little for those that oppose your ideals and values, as long as they don't become an immediate threat. 

**Skill Training** You become trained in Athletics or Acrobatics.

**Skill Feat** You gain the *Quick Jump* skill feat if you chose Atheltics, you gain the *Cat Fall* skill feat if you chose Acrobatics.

**Additional Spell** You add *true strike* to your spell repertoire. 

### Minor Ideal Benefits: Zeal 

You unleash a string of zealous propaganda, laced with subtle enchantments, at a creature you can see. Stirring it to attack you, and only you. You gain the *zealous derision* focus spell. A focus spell that goads your enemies to attack you, or take a penalty to their attack rolls.

# Inquisitions
 
Inquisitions not only grant an increase to your skills, or give you additional skill feats. They also bestow upon you a special judgement spell. These can vary from strong shields, to healing effect, or even damage. 
 
You gain an Initial Inquisition when you reach 5th level, an Advanced Inquistion on 11th level, and a True Inquisition on 17th level. 

 
You can choose to downgrade and take a Initial Inquisition for your Advanced Inquisition, or an Advanced Inquisition for your True inquisition. But you can not upgrade, even when you are retraining at a later level. You can only choose an Advanced Inquisition for your Advanced or True Inquisition class feature, but not with your Initial Inquisition for example.

*Feat 1*
*Traits: Inquisitor, Investigator*

# Class Archetypes

> # Class Archetype Reminder
> Archetypes with the class trait fundamentally diverge from your class's specialties but still fit within the theme of your class.
>
> You can select a class archetype only if your class meets the criteria listed in the archetype's prerequisites. Class archetypes always alter or replace some of a class's static class features in addition to any new feats they offer.
>

Below you will find 2 class archetypes, each of these is considered rare and requires GM approval. The Living Grimoire turns you into a spellcaster, while the Omdura gives you access to a special replacement for baleful bane, and grants you multiple support options. 

# Living Grimoire Ideal

The living grimoire literally wields the sacred word of his deity, using his holy tome to smite the foes of his god with divine might. Unlike most inquisitors, a living grimoire focuses on careful study of divine scripture, valuing knowledge over intuition. If you choose this class archetype, you must select Living Grimoire Dedication as your 2nd-level class feat. 

**Prerequisites:** You must be an inquisitor

**Skill Training** You become trained in Arcana.

**Skill Feat** You gain the *Recognize Spell* skill feat.

**Additional Spell** You add *telekinetic projectile* to your spell repertoire. 

**Living Grimoire Adjustments:** You use Intelligence for your spellcasting modifier instead of Wisdom. Your spellcasting also works different, instead of the Divine Spellcasting and Spell Repertoire class features, you gain the Living Grimoire Spellcasting class feature at 1st level and you also don't gain the Unlimited Signatures Spells class feature at 3th level. When you gain the 15th level class feature, Stalwart, you can freely choose between Reflex or Fortitude. 

### Minor Ideal Benefits: Grimoire

A living grimoire forms a supernatural bond with a large ironbound tome containing the holy text of his deity and learns to use it as a weapon. When wielding the holy book as a weapon it acts as the same as a cold-iron Mace. It can be upgraded, can be etched with runes, or affixed with talismans as a normal mace would be able to, and also serves as a divine focus.

## Living Grimoire Spellcasting

Your Deity and ideal provides you with divine magical power. You can cast spells using the Cast a Spell activity, and you can supply material, somatic, and verbal components when casting spells. Because you're an inquisitor, you can draw replacement sigils with the tip of your weapon or your free hand for spells requiring material components, replacing them with somatic components instead of needing a material component pouch.

At 1st level, you can prepare one 1st-level spell and five cantrips each morning from the spells in your holy book (see below). Prepared spells remain available to you until you cast them or until you prepare your spells again. The number of spells you can prepare is called your spell slots.

As you increase in level as a Living Grimoire inquisitor, your number of spell slots and the highest level of spells you can cast from spell slots increase, shown in Table 1–1: Inquisitor Spells per Day. Because you split your focus between physical training and magical scholarship, you have no more than two spell slots of your highest level and, if you can cast 2nd-level spells or higher, two spell slots of 1 level lower than your highest spell level.

Some of your spells require you to attempt a spell attack roll to see how effective they are, or have your enemies roll against your spell DC (typically by attempting a saving throw). Your spell attack rolls and spell DCs use your Intelligence modifier.

### Heightening Spells

When you get spell slots of 2nd level and higher, you can fill those slots with stronger versions of lower-level spells. This increases the spell's level, heightening it to match the spell slot. Many spells have specific improvements when they're heightened to certain levels.

### Cantrips

A cantrip is a special type of spell that doesn't use spell slots. You can cast a cantrip at will, any number of times per day. A cantrip is always automatically heightened to half your level rounded up—this is usually equal to the highest level of spell you can cast as a Living Grimoire inquisitor. For example, as a 1st-level Living Grimoire inquisitor, your cantrips are 1st-level spells, and as a 5th-level Living Grimoire inquisitor, your cantrips are 3rd-level spells.

### Holy Book 

Your holy book works like a spellbook. Every divine spell has a written version, usually recorded in a spellbook or scroll. You start with a holy book worth 10 sp or less, which you receive for free and must study to prepare your spells each day. The holy book contains your choice of eight divine cantrips and four 1st-level divine spells. You choose these from the common spells on the divine spell list or from other divine spells you gain access to.

Each time you gain a level, you add two more divine spells to your holy book, of any level of spell you can cast. You can also use the Religion skill to add other spells that you find in your adventures (see Learn a Spell). Though you lose some lower spell slots as you increase in level, you keep the spells in your spellbook and can prepare them in your higher-level slots as normal.

The holy book does not count as an arcane spell book, as such if you have a spellbook from multiple sources (such as a Wizard Dedication feat), you can not use the same spellbook for all your spells. As these focus on two different magical traditions.

# FAQ

---

**Q: Why not make this a cleric doctrine, or class archetype?**
A: I originally wanted to make a homebrew with class archetypes for the cleric to create sprititual successors for the 1e warpriest and inquisitor. But unlike the battlepriest, my warpriest variant, I quickly realized that the inquisitor came with a lot more baggage and I couldn't simply dump some rogue-lite abilities on the cleric chassis. 

I had to account for the limited spell casting, the smiting, the teamwork feats, the judgements, bane, etc... Each of these on it's own would make for an easy archetype, but when trying to do them all simultaneously it quickly turned out to be easier to start from a new job, instead of trying to cram everything in a single class archetype. 

This also allowed me to cover a lot more material. Archetypes like the Living Grimoire or Omdura wouldn't have been possible otherwise. 

---

**Q: What was your design goal making this?**
A: I wanted to create a spiritual successor for the inquisitor in Pathfinder 2e. The inquisitor in 1e was a divine themed investigator, that had some affinity with tracking, intimidation, teamwork  debuffing and could smite on top of it. Those where the core principle's I wanted to port over to 2nd edition, but without stepping to much on the design space of the rogue, cleric and even magus. 

To reach this goal I specifically honed in on the reactivity aspect of rogue, blended it with a dash of swashbuckler for some Intimidation, ranger for tracking and hunting, and cleric for spell casting. My biggest hurdle was how to integrate Bane as something more than a one-off ability, and how to make Divine Smite feel different from the magus' Spellstrike, or the cleric' Channel Smite. 

---

**Q: How fateful is this to the 1e Inquisitor? Are all 1e domains and inquisitions here?**
A: It's important to know that I set out with the idea of creating a spiritual successor, not a one-to-one conversion. Pathfinder 1e and 2e are vastly different systems, build on entirely different assumptions, a simple port was impossible. What I did was take a look at what 1e had to offer, and think how I could replicate something, that could fill that hole. My original version of the class was way more closely aligned with its 1e counterpart, but the content bloat was it's number 1 criticism.

---

**Q: Wait, wasn't this different last time I saw it?**
A: You most likely saw v1.0 of the class, which was kind of a personal playtest. After gathering feedback from the community I updated the class to what you see now. 

---

**Q: How balanced is this homebrew?**
A: Good question, I'm still trying to run the numbers on this. The class chassis itself is perfectly balanced, it might even be a bit weak compared to the CRB classes, but some of focus spells are really, **really** good. I'll be sure to adjust them whenever I have a more conclusive answer.
